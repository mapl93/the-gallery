## Overview

Rich Text Section presents a passive, bounded narrative module inside a larger page. It owns an optional visible title, one required target-owned semantic body, readable measure, and section rhythm.

With a title, the visible contextual heading names a native `section`. Without a title, use a generic `div` instead of an unnamed section or autonomous `article`. Missing required body content omits the complete component.

The body slot retains target-owned paragraphs, contextual headings, ordered and unordered lists, emphasis, quotations and citations, figures and captions, images, and native links. The component constrains presentation without flattening document semantics or importing the broader Article Body profile.

## Usage Guidelines

<DoDont>
  <Do>Use semantic document elements and a clear contextual heading hierarchy for a bounded page narrative.</Do>
  <Dont>Do not use S15 as a complete article, rich-text editor, sanitizer, or substitute for Article Body.</Dont>
</DoDont>

Keep the content concise enough to remain a page section. Use Article Body when the content is independently publishable or needs drop caps, callouts, code, tables, full-bleed media, or product embeds.

## Accessibility

- Preserve a logical heading order within the surrounding page.
- Use native lists, quotations, links, and media semantics inside the body.
- Give informative images useful alternative text and decorative images empty alternatives.
- Keep link purpose understandable out of context and preserve visible focus.
- Omit the complete component when no semantic body is available; do not leave an empty shell.
- Keep source trust, sanitization, link policy, legal copy, and media loading in the owning target.
